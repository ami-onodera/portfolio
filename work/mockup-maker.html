Mockup maker: from a prospect’s client website to our portal

I built a guided mockup maker that lets Sales and Customer Success teams turn any prospect’s website into a realistic, on-brand preview of a referral portal. No designer, no design tool knowledge, nothing touching production and nothing on the page the product can’t actually deliver.

Role
Concept, system design, build and rollout
For
Sales, Customer Success and the Integrations team
Tools
Claude (custom skills), HTML/CSS, Contentful, GitLab Pages
Output
One self-contained HTML file, a deck slide or a private link
the-pain-point.txt_▢x

The pain point

Prospects couldn’t picture the product

I work at a B2B referral-marketing platform. Our partners embed a branded referral portal where their customers invite friends and collect rewards. Every portal runs on the same structure, but it is dressed in the partner’s colours, type and imagery, so it feels like part of the partner’s own site.

That is hard to explain in a sales call. Prospects kept asking the same thing: “What would this look like for us?” The honest answer was a generic demo in someone else’s colours, which undersold the product at the moment that mattered most. Getting a proper mockup had three problems:

bottleneck.txt_▢x
Bottleneck

It needed a designer

Every mockup went through someone with design skills and a Figma seat. Turnaround took days, so most deals never got one.

risk.txt_▢x
Risk

Production was off-limits

Building a real portal in the CMS before a contract was signed wasn’t an option. Prospects couldn’t touch live infrastructure.

trust.txt_▢x
Trust

Mockups over-promised

Freehand designs drifted from what the platform could build. A prospect who signs expecting a section that doesn’t exist is a problem someone has to fix later.

How might we let non-designers produce a believable, buildable preview of a prospect’s portal before a contract is signed?

The insight: only the skin changes

Having a defined structure of a portal is the key. If the structure is fixed, a mockup is just a set of brand elements applied to one template. Pick a brand below and watch the same portal change skin.

How it came together

A mockup that shows something we can’t build costs more than no mockup at all. So most of the design work went into what the tool refuses to do.

step-2-build.txt_▢x
2 · Build

One template, one file

I rebuilt the live portal as a single standalone HTML page, section for section, with every visual decision as a CSS variable. A rebrand means changing a handful of values, not rewriting the layout.

I picked HTML over a design tool on purpose. One file emails cleanly, opens anywhere and feels live: buttons react, “show more” expands, the email field validates. Figma would have needed a seat for every user.

Delivered as a skill, shared the way teams work

Everything above is packaged as a custom Claude skill: a written playbook the AI agent follows, with templates and helper scripts. Nobody opens a CSS file or picks a hex code. They paste a prospect’s website URL and answer four questions. The skill reads the site, shows the palette for confirmation before building, runs its own checks and hands over the result in whatever shape the team needs.
